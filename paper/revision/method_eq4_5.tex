\subsection{Adaptive Cosine Schedule $\beta(t)$}
In early rounds, the locally updated models produce high-variance
predictions on $\mathcal{V}$, and each score $\kappa_k^t$ is estimated from
only 50 images. The aggregation weights are therefore moved gradually from
sample-count weighting towards quality-driven weighting by a half-cosine
schedule:
\begin{equation}
  \beta(t) = \beta_0 \cos\!\left(\frac{\pi (t-1)}{2(T-1)}\right),
  \label{eq:beta}
\end{equation}
where $\beta_0 = 0.5$ and $T = 10$. In the first round, sample counts and
server-set quality contribute equally ($\beta(1) = 0.5$). By the final
round, $\beta(T) = 0$, and the weights are determined by the quality scores
alone.

\subsection{QWK-Weighted Aggregation}
The server-set scores are converted into quality weights by a
temperature-scaled softmax, applied after a lower floor $\kappa_{\min}$:
\begin{equation}
  q_k^t = \frac{\exp\!\big(\max(\kappa_k^t, \kappa_{\min}) / \tau\big)}
               {\sum_{j=1}^{K} \exp\!\big(\max(\kappa_j^t, \kappa_{\min}) / \tau\big)},
  \label{eq:quality}
\end{equation}
where $\kappa_{\min} = 0.1$ and $\tau = 3$. The aggregation weight of client
$k$ in round $t$ is obtained by blending $q_k^t$ with the sample-count share:
\begin{equation}
  \omega_k^t = \beta(t)\,\frac{n_k}{\sum_{j=1}^{K} n_j}
             + \big(1 - \beta(t)\big)\, q_k^t ,
  \label{eq:weights}
\end{equation}
and the global model is updated as
$w^{t+1} = \sum_{k=1}^{K} \omega_k^t \, \tilde{w}_k^t$
(Algorithm~\ref{alg:ordinalfed}). A minimum share of $\omega_{\min} = 0.03$
per client, followed by renormalisation, is additionally enforced as a
safeguard. With $\beta_0 = 0.5$ and $K = 5$, this floor never binds, because
every client already receives at least $(1-\beta_0)\,q_{\min} \approx 0.08$.

The softmax and the floor on $\kappa$ make the scheme deliberately
conservative. No client is excluded from aggregation, since a single
50-image estimate of $\kappa_k^t$ is too noisy to justify discarding an
entire hospital's update. With $\tau = 3$ and $\kappa \in [0.1, 1]$, the
quality term can change a client's relative weight by at most a factor of
$e^{0.9/3} \approx 1.35$. The aggregation therefore acts as a bounded
re-weighting that moves away from pure sample-count proportionality, rather
than as a hard filter on client updates.
