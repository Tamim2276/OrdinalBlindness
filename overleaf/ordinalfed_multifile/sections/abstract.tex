\begin{abstract}
Diabetic Retinopathy (DR) is a leading cause of preventable blindness. With
federated learning (FL), hospitals can train one DR grading model together without
sharing retinal images. When the hospitals see very different patients, however,
the standard FedAvg method can collapse. This paper studies \textbf{OrdinalFed},
which has two parts. The first is an \emph{OrdinalLoss} that penalises a wrong
grade by its squared distance from the true grade. The second is a
\emph{QWK-weighted aggregation}: the server scores each client model by its
Quadratic Weighted Kappa (QWK) on 50 held-out images, and over the rounds it moves
the aggregation weights from image counts towards these scores. All experiments on
the DDR dataset were repeated with three seeds and scored on the held-out test
split. Under extreme label skew (Dirichlet $\alpha=0.1$), FedAvg collapsed in every
seed, with a worst-round test QWK of 0.378. OrdinalFed raised the worst round to
0.514, with no change at the hospitals and about 1.2~s of extra server time per
round. The ablations show where this gain comes from: small hospitals get more
weight. Equal client weights did even better (0.630), while the QWK scores and the
OrdinalLoss added no measurable benefit. FedProx was the most stable method
(0.641), and OrdinalFed with FedProx's proximal term came close to it (0.627). On
the IID and realistic multi-site partitions, all methods were within 0.01 QWK of
each other.
\end{abstract}
