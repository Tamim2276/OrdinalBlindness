\section{Conclusion}

This paper evaluated OrdinalFed, a federated method for multi-grade Diabetic
Retinopathy screening that combines a distance-aware OrdinalLoss with QWK-weighted
aggregation. All experiments used the DDR dataset, three seeds and a held-out test
split, and the trained models were also tested on the external APTOS 2019 dataset,
where they scored close to the centralised model. Under extreme label skew, OrdinalFed's server-side weighting prevents most
of FedAvg's collapse (worst-round test QWK 0.514 versus 0.378) without extra
communication. The ablations show why: small hospitals get more weight. Equal
client weights work even better, a 50-image server set is too small for the QWK
scores to rank clients, and the OrdinalLoss does not help in the federated setting.
FedProx remains the most stable method, and adding its proximal term to OrdinalFed
comes close to FedProx but does not beat it. On realistic multi-site and IID
partitions, all methods perform about the same. Future work will test larger
server sets, higher image resolution and more external datasets, and will look at
formal differential privacy guarantees.
