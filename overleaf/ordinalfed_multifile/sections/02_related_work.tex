\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Ordinal learning for DR.}
Standard multi-class losses treat severity grades as separate categories and ignore
their order. Toledo-Cort\'{e}s et al.~\cite{toledocortes2022dqor} proposed Deep
Quantum Ordinal Regression (DQOR) and reported a grading mean absolute error (MAE)
of 0.2871 on EyePACS. Polat et al.~\cite{polat2024cdwce} proposed Class Distance
Weighted Cross-Entropy (CDW-CE), which scales the loss by the distance between
classes, and reached a QWK of 0.8568 for ulcerative colitis grading. Both studies
show that distance-aware losses help in centralised training. As far as the
authors know, such losses have not yet been combined with quality-weighted
aggregation in federated DR grading.

\textbf{Federated learning in ophthalmology.}
FedAvg~\cite{mcmahan2017fedavg} is the standard FL algorithm.
FedProx~\cite{li2020fedprox} adds an $L_2$ proximal term that keeps local weights
close to the global model when client data differ. For DR, Bhulakshmi and
Rajput~\cite{bhulakshmi2024feddl} proposed FedDL, which switches between
aggregation rules during training, and Ciaramella et al.~\cite{ciaramella2025fl}
combined federated DR screening with Grad-CAM explanations across 20 non-IID
clients on APTOS~2019. These methods treat grading as plain (nominal)
classification, and they weight clients by dataset size rather than by how good
their models are.

\textbf{Quality-aware aggregation.}
Weighting clients by sample count can fail when client data are strongly biased or
deliberately corrupted. Bejenar et al.~\cite{bejenar2023aggregation} scored client
models on a server-side validation set (FedLasso) and beat FedAvg by 45.6~pp under
adversarial label flipping. Li et al.~\cite{li2023revisiting} showed that client
weights that do not sum to one act as an implicit regulariser (FedLAW), and Li et
al.~\cite{li2025fedaaw} proposed FedAAW, which sets aggregation weights from
gradient norms and improved non-IID accuracy by 37.32~pp. A review by Mora et
al.~\cite{mora2024review} concluded that client-side regularisation combined with
server-side adaptive aggregation gives the most reliable results on non-IID data.
OrdinalFed follows this line by pairing an ordinal client loss with QWK-based
server aggregation.
