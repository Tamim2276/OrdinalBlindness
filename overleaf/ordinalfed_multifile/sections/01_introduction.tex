\section{Introduction}

Diabetic Retinopathy (DR) is the primary microvascular complication
of diabetes mellitus and the leading cause of preventable blindness
among working-age adults globally. The disease is characterised by
progressive damage to the retinal blood vessels, presenting visually
as microaneurysms, haemorrhages, hard exudates, and in severe cases,
neovascularisation. In developing nations such as Bangladesh, where over
13.1~million individuals live with diabetes~\cite{idf2021}, the clinical
impact of DR is intensified by a severe deficit of retinal specialists
outside major urban centres. Clinically, DR progresses along a strict
five-grade ordinal scale: Grade~0 (No DR), Grade~1 (Mild Non-Proliferative
DR, NPDR), Grade~2 (Moderate NPDR), Grade~3 (Severe NPDR), and Grade~4
(Proliferative DR). Timely identification of referable disease (Grade~$\geq$~2)
is paramount to prevent permanent vision loss, making automated screening
systems highly desirable.

In centralised environments, deep convolutional neural networks achieve
expert-level diagnostic precision. On the highly imbalanced DDR dataset~\cite{li2019ddr},
an EfficientNet-B3 architecture achieves a Quadratic Weighted Kappa
(QWK) of 0.8494. % TODO(Day 4): new centralised QWK (results/centralised_test.json)
QWK serves as the clinical benchmark metric for
ordinal grading because it penalises misclassifications quadratically
relative to grade distance: misclassifying a Grade~4 proliferative
retinopathy image as Grade~0 incurs a drastically higher penalty than
predicting Grade~3. However, centralising patient fundus photographs
from multiple healthcare institutions violates strict medical data
privacy regulations, such as HIPAA and GDPR. Federated Learning (FL)~\cite{mcmahan2017fedavg}
addresses this fundamental challenge by training a shared global model across
distributed hospitals via iterative weight exchanges, keeping raw patient
records strictly localised.

The primary algorithmic obstacle in clinical FL is \emph{statistical
heterogeneity}, commonly referred to as Non-IID data. Retinal patient
distributions vary drastically depending on the hospital tier: community
screening clinics observe overwhelmingly healthy (Grade~0) cohorts,
tertiary referral centres encounter high concentrations of severe
proliferative cases (Grades~3--4), and rural primary clinics observe
intermediate presentations. Under extreme Dirichlet skew ($\alpha=0.1$),
standard FedAvg is observed to undergo catastrophic forgetting, crashing
from a peak QWK of 0.7993 down to 0.4064 in a single communication round.
Proximal methods such as FedProx~\cite{li2020fedprox} provide partial
regularisation against local weight divergence but remain highly
susceptible to severe degradation (dropping to 0.5963).

Crucially,
both of these foundational methods aggregate parameters proportionally to
client sample counts, allowing large screening centres with massive but
monochromatic Grade~0 cohorts to dominate aggregation and heavily distort
the global ordinal decision boundaries.

Furthermore, standard Cross-Entropy loss is fundamentally
\emph{ordinal-blind}: it penalises all non-diagonal misclassifications
equally regardless of clinical severity. While ordinal loss
formulations~\cite{toledocortes2022dqor}--[\citen{polat2024cdwce}] have proven
highly effective in single-institution settings for guiding feature
extraction, their capacity to regularise local client drift within
federated networks has not been explored.

To address these challenges, \textbf{OrdinalFed} is proposed: a federated
framework designed specifically for ordinal medical imaging. Its two key
contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Distance-Aware OrdinalLoss:} A differentiable hybrid
  objective combining class-weighted Cross-Entropy with a squared
  expected-grade penalty to explicitly suppress severe, multi-grade
  misclassifications during local client training.
  \item \textbf{QWK-Weighted Quality Aggregation with Adaptive $\beta$:}
  A server-side scoring protocol evaluating client models on a balanced
  50-image proxy server set $\mathcal{V}$. This mechanism gradually
  shifts aggregation weights from sample-count proportionality towards
  ordinal diagnostic quality via a cosine-annealing schedule, limiting the
  influence of clients whose updates degrade on $\mathcal{V}$.
\end{enumerate}
