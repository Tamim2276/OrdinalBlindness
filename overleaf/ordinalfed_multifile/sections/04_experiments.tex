\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Dataset and Preprocessing}

All frameworks are evaluated on the \textbf{DDR benchmark dataset}~\cite{li2019ddr},
which contains 13,673 fundus photographs, 12,522 of them gradable. Excluding the
ungradable images leaves 6,260 training, 2,503 validation, and 3,759 test images.
All images are resized to $224 \times 224$ pixels. During local training, random
horizontal flips, rotations of up to $\pm 15^{\circ}$, and mild colour jitter are
applied to reduce overfitting. The training set displays heavy natural class
imbalance: Grade~0 (50.0\%), Grade~1 (5.0\%), Grade~2 (35.8\%), Grade~3 (1.9\%),
and Grade~4 (7.3\%).

\subsection{Modeling Clinical Heterogeneity}

$K = 5$ clients are instantiated under three statistical scenarios, shown in
Fig.~\ref{fig:distributions}. In the figure, each bar represents one client: its
segments give the share of each DR grade among that client's images, shaded from
light (Grade~0) to dark (Grade~4), and the number under each client label is the
client's total image count.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,noitemsep,topsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{IID Partition:} Uniform random assignment (1,252 samples per
  client). This serves as the homogeneous upper bound for FL.
  \item \textbf{Dirichlet Non-IID ($\alpha=0.1$):} Pathological skew generated
  via a Dirichlet distribution $p_k \sim \mathrm{Dir}(\alpha)$. An $\alpha=0.1$
  forces each client to draw samples almost exclusively from a single class.
  Consequently, client sizes range from 32 to 2,087 samples; Client~0 contains
  solely 32 images (100\% Grade~0), while Client~4 holds 2,087 images dominated
  (98\%) by Grade~2.
  \item \textbf{Real-Site Clinical Partition:} Data are distributed according to
  fixed grade profiles that model five clinical archetypes, yielding: Site~0
  (general screening clinic; 40\% of images, 84\% Grade~0), Site~1 (rural primary
  care; 22\%, mainly Grades~0 and~2, with the largest Grade~1 share at 11\%),
  Site~2 (specialist referral eye hospital; 10\%, the largest Grade~3--4 share at
  38\%), Site~3 (diabetic retinopathy clinic; 19\%, 67\% Grade~2), and Site~4
  (urban hospital; 9\%, mixed grades).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Network Architecture and Baselines}

All federated experiments use an EfficientNet-B3 backbone, chosen for its balance
between parameter efficiency and feature extraction capability at
$224 \times 224$ resolution. To simulate real-world deployment where pre-trained
weights are adapted, all FL runs are warm-started from a centralised model trained
for 30 epochs (validation QWK = 0.8494).
% TODO(Day 4): new centralised validation QWK (results/centralised_done.json)
OrdinalFed is benchmarked against:
(1)~\textbf{Centralised Baseline}; (2)~\textbf{FedAvg}~\cite{mcmahan2017fedavg};
(3)~\textbf{FedProx}~\cite{li2020fedprox} with proximal coefficient $\mu = 0.01$; and
(4)~\textbf{AccWt}, an accuracy-weighted aggregation baseline in the spirit of
server-side validation scoring~\cite{bejenar2023aggregation}. AccWt uses the same
schedule and softmax weighting as OrdinalFed (Eqs.~\ref{eq:beta}--\ref{eq:weights})
but scores each client by plain accuracy on $\mathcal{V}$ instead of QWK, and its
clients train with class-weighted cross-entropy.
All methods execute $T = 10$ communication rounds with $E = 5$ local epochs,
batch size 64, Adam optimiser ($\eta = 10^{-4}$), and the same class-imbalance
weights $\mathbf{c}$.

\subsection{Ablations and Evaluation Protocol}

To isolate the contribution of each component, three ablations of OrdinalFed are
evaluated: (i)~OrdinalLoss with sample-count aggregation; (ii)~class-weighted
cross-entropy with QWK-weighted aggregation; and (iii)~class-weighted cross-entropy
with uniform aggregation ($\omega_k^t = 1/K$).

Every configuration is repeated with three random seeds, which control data order
and augmentation, and results are reported as mean $\pm$ standard deviation.
% TODO(Day 4): confirm seed counts match what finished (run_queue.py --status).
After each round, the global model is evaluated on the DDR validation split and on
the DDR test split excluding the 50 images of $\mathcal{V}$ (3,709 images). For each
run, the reported model is the one from the round with the highest validation QWK.
The test split is never used for training, aggregation, or model selection, and
therefore provides an independent estimate of performance.
