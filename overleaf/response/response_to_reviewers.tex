\textit{OrdinalFed: Federated Learning with Ordinal-Aware Aggregation for Diabetic Retinopathy Grading}
\end{center}

We thank both reviewers for their careful reading and helpful comments. Each
point is answered below. Section, table and figure numbers refer to the revised
manuscript.

\textbf{Reviewer \#2:} \textit{``\ldots stronger experimental validation through
ablation studies, additional recent baselines, independent validation data,
repeated experiments, and detailed class-wise performance analysis\ldots''}

{\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}L{3.1cm}L{11.0cm}L{2.4cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Request} & \textbf{What we changed} & \textbf{Where} \\
\midrule
Ablation studies &
We removed one component at a time, under $\alpha=0.1$ and the real-site
partition: OrdinalLoss with image-count weights, cross-entropy with QWK weights,
cross-entropy with equal weights, and the full method. We also tested three
settings of OrdinalFed ($\lambda$, $\beta_0$, $\tau$), fixed before the runs and
compared on validation data only. The ablation shows that the aggregation, not
OrdinalLoss, prevents FedAvg's collapse, and that equal client weights work at
least as well as QWK weights. &
Sec.~IV-D, V-C, Table~III \\
Additional baselines &
We added AccWt, a quality-weighted baseline based on server-side validation
scoring [9]. It is identical to our aggregation except that clients are scored by
accuracy instead of QWK, so it shows whether the ordinal score itself matters. It
performs almost the same as QWK weighting. We also added OrdinalFed + Prox, which
combines our aggregation with FedProx's proximal term. &
Sec.~IV-C, Tables~I--II \\
Independent validation data &
All main results now come from the DDR test split (3,709 images, excluding the
50 server-set images). These images are never used for training, aggregation or
choosing the reported round, which is selected on the validation split. All
models score lower on the test split; this is now discussed in the Limitations. &
Sec.~IV-D, Table~I \\
Repeated experiments &
Every configuration was run with three random seeds; results are reported as
mean $\pm$ standard deviation. With three seeds, the late FedProx drop seen in the
single run of the submitted version did not recur. &
Tables~I--IV \\
Class-wise analysis &
We now report per-grade recall and F1 on the test split for FedAvg, FedProx and
OrdinalFed. No method detects Grade~1 reliably (recall $\leq 0.05$); OrdinalFed
has the highest Grade~3 recall in both partitions. &
Sec.~V-D, Table~IV \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
}

\textit{How the conclusions changed:} the repeated test-split experiments requested
by Reviewer~\#2 changed our conclusions, and the abstract, introduction, results and
conclusion now state them as measured. OrdinalFed still prevents most of FedAvg's
collapse under extreme skew (worst-round test QWK 0.514 versus 0.378), but the gain
comes from giving small hospitals more weight rather than from the QWK scores or
OrdinalLoss, and FedProx is the most stable method. We believe this is a more useful
result for the community than the single-run comparison in the submitted version.

\textit{Other corrections:} Eqs.~(4)--(6) now describe exactly the aggregation used
in the experiments. The analysis of why the aggregation is stable (Sec.~V-C) was
rewritten from the per-round server scores and aggregation weights logged during
training. The server-side overhead is now the measured value (1.2\,s per round),
and the dataset statistics in Sec.~IV-A were corrected.
