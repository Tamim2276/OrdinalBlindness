\section{Conclusion}

In this paper, OrdinalFed, a federated method for multi-grade Diabetic Retinopathy
screening that combines a distance-aware OrdinalLoss with QWK-weighted aggregation,
has been evaluated. All experiments were run on the DDR dataset with three seeds and
a held-out test split, and the trained models were also tested on the external
APTOS 2019 dataset, where they scored close to the centralised model. Under extreme
label skew, OrdinalFed's server-side weighting prevents most of FedAvg's collapse
(worst-round test QWK 0.514 versus 0.378) without extra communication. The
ablations show why: small hospitals are given more weight. Equal client weights
work even better, a 50-image server set is too small for the QWK scores to rank
clients, and the OrdinalLoss does not help in the federated setting. FedProx
remains the most stable method, and adding its proximal term to OrdinalFed comes
close to FedProx but does not beat it. On realistic multi-site and IID partitions,
all methods perform about the same. In future work, larger server sets, higher
image resolution and more external datasets will be tested, and formal differential
privacy guarantees will be studied.
